# Clinical research interpretation and critical appraisal

## Interpret results in layers
1. **Design validity:** Was the study design appropriate for the question?
2. **Internal validity:** Could bias, confounding, missing data or measurement error explain the result?
3. **Effect magnitude:** What is the absolute and relative effect, in which direction, and over what follow-up?
4. **Precision:** What range of effects is compatible with the data? Are confidence intervals wide enough to include important benefit or harm?
5. **Clinical importance:** Does the estimate cross a prespecified clinically meaningful threshold?
6. **Applicability:** Do participants, setting, intervention, comparator and outcome match the target decision?
7. **Certainty:** How confident should readers be in the body of evidence across studies?

## Absolute and relative measures
- Risk = events / participants at risk over a stated period.
- Risk difference = intervention risk minus control risk.
- Relative risk = intervention risk / control risk.
- Odds ratio = odds in intervention / odds in control; it is not interchangeable with RR.
- ARR for benefit = control risk minus intervention risk.
- NNT = ceiling(1 / ARR), only when ARR is positive and expressed as a proportion. Always state outcome and time horizon.
- NNH = ceiling(1 / absolute risk increase), when intervention risk is higher.
- A point-estimate NNT/NNH can be misleading when the risk-difference CI crosses zero; report uncertainty and avoid presenting a single number as definitive.

## Diagnostic accuracy
- Sensitivity = TP / (TP + FN).
- Specificity = TN / (TN + FP).
- PPV = TP / (TP + FP); NPV = TN / (TN + FN).
- PPV/NPV change with prevalence. Transporting them requires an appropriate target prevalence and stable sensitivity/specificity.
- LR+ = sensitivity / (1 - specificity); LR− = (1 - sensitivity) / specificity.
- AUC needs patient-level continuous/ordinal test scores and cannot be recovered from one 2×2 table.
- Accuracy alone does not prove clinical utility; consider consequences, thresholds, pathway and patient outcomes.

## Appraisal frameworks: domain prompts, not automated scoring
- Randomized trials: consider the RoB 2 domains (randomization, deviations, missing outcomes, outcome measurement, selective reporting).
- Non-randomized intervention studies: consider ROBINS-I domains, especially confounding and participant selection.
- Diagnostic accuracy: consider QUADAS-2 domains (patient selection, index test, reference standard, flow/timing).
- Body of evidence: GRADE considers risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias.

The included interactive checklist is an educational prompt set. It is not an official reproduction of these tools and must not produce a final risk-of-bias or certainty rating without trained reviewer judgment.

## Reporting template
State study design, population, setting, intervention/exposure, comparator, outcome definition, follow-up, analysis population, missingness, effect estimate, confidence interval, p-value (if relevant), absolute baseline risk, limitations and applicability. Separate prespecified from exploratory analyses.
