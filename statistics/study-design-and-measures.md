# Study design and epidemiological measures reference

| Design | Typical sampling frame | Common measure | Main interpretation caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cross-sectional | Sampled at a point/period | Prevalence | Temporality is often unclear |
| Cohort | Exposed/unexposed followed over time | Incidence, risk ratio, rate ratio, hazard ratio | Confounding, loss to follow-up, time origin |
| Case-control | Sampled by outcome status | Odds ratio | Cannot directly estimate incidence from ordinary case-control sampling |
| Randomised trial | Allocation by randomisation | Intention-to-treat contrast | Allocation concealment, adherence, missing outcomes, generalisability |

## Core formulas
- Prevalence = existing cases / population at the specified time or period.
- Cumulative incidence (risk) = new cases during a defined period / population at risk at baseline.
- Incidence rate = new events / person-time at risk.
- Risk difference = risk in exposed − risk in reference.
- Risk ratio = risk in exposed / risk in reference.
- Odds ratio = (a×d)/(b×c) for a 2×2 table.
- Number needed to treat/harm = 1 / absolute risk reduction/increase, only when the effect and time horizon are meaningful and the estimate is sufficiently credible.
- Sensitivity = TP/(TP+FN); specificity = TN/(TN+FP).
- PPV = TP/(TP+FP); NPV = TN/(TN+FN). Predictive values depend on prevalence.
- Likelihood ratio positive = sensitivity/(1−specificity); negative = (1−sensitivity)/specificity.

Always state population, numerator, denominator, time window, unit, missingness and reference group.
