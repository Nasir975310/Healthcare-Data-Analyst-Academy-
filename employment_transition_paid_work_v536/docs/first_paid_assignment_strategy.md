# First paid assignment strategy

## Suitable initial scope
Start with low-risk, bounded work: spreadsheet cleanup on de-identified business data, data dictionary creation, duplicate/missingness profiling, SQL report correction, or a reproducible descriptive summary. Avoid independent clinical decision support, patient-level risk scoring, claims of regulatory compliance, and handling identifiable health data without formal authorization and safeguards.

## Discovery questions
1. What decision will this report support?
2. What is the row-level grain and source of each file?
3. Who owns the data and authorizes access?
4. Is the dataset de-identified, and what privacy/security rules apply?
5. What exact period, population, exclusions and metric definitions are required?
6. What deliverable format and acceptance checks are expected?
7. Who signs off, and what is explicitly out of scope?

## INR planning examples — not market rates
Illustrative effort-based arithmetic only:
- 3 hours × ₹400/hour = ₹1,200.
- 8 hours × ₹500/hour = ₹4,000.
- 15 hours × ₹600/hour = ₹9,000.

These are example calculations, not a recommended market price or earnings promise. Set a rate only after estimating complexity, revisions, platform fees, taxes, communication, security and opportunity cost. For a first project, a small fixed-scope paid pilot can reduce uncertainty.

## Scam and risk screen
- Reject requests to pay a registration/security fee to obtain work.
- Verify company domain, named contact, business presence and contract authority independently.
- Never share OTPs, passwords, API keys, bank login or remote-control access.
- Avoid cheque overpayment/refund schemes, crypto-only payment, reshipping and unpaid large “tests”.
- Do not install unknown executables or browser extensions from recruiters.
- Keep scope, milestones, acceptance and payment terms in writing.
- Use platform escrow only through the platform's official workflow; verify withdrawal/payment terms.

## 4-week execution target
Week 1: finalize one case study, CV and GitHub README; reproduce project from a clean clone.  
Week 2: submit 8–12 carefully matched applications; ask two domain contacts for feedback.  
Week 3: complete two timed take-homes and one mock interview; submit another 8–12 targeted applications.  
Week 4: review response rates, improve weak evidence, follow up once where appropriate, and pitch only tightly scoped, safe freelance tasks.

Track outputs and response rates. These targets are activity goals, not guarantees of employment.
